# Final challenge explanation — why this example is structured this way

## Why there's only one worked example for three scenarios

The final challenge is intentionally open — three different scenarios, no single correct artifact. Providing one fully worked example (rather than three) is deliberate: showing the same team the *shape* of a complete answer once is more useful than three shallow ones, and copying the specific scenario would defeat the point of the challenge, which is choosing and defending your own.

## Why the brief reuses Lab 1's five fields

By the final challenge, you've already practiced writing Context/Outcome/Inputs/Constraints/Quality bar in Lab 1 and using acceptance criteria as a definition of done throughout. The final challenge doesn't introduce a new brief format — it asks you to apply the same one to something you're building yourself, which is the actual test of whether the earlier labs stuck.

## Why the artifact reuses Lab 3's dataset instead of inventing new data

Building on data you've already independently verified (via `analyze.py`) means the verification step in this challenge is real, not decorative — you can check the new artifact's numbers against a script whose logic you already walked through in Lab 3, instead of trusting a brand-new, unverified calculation.

## Why the reflection is graded as seriously as the artifact

A working script with a confident-sounding readout, but no stated assumptions, is the same failure mode the whole workshop has been warning about: fluent output that hides its own uncertainty. The reflection is where you demonstrate that you know what your own artifact does and doesn't establish — which is the actual skill this workshop is teaching, more than any specific tool.
