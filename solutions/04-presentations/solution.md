# Lab 4 — Example solution

One valid five-slide story arc for briefing college leadership on the workshop registration data (using the numbers from Lab 3's `analyze.py` output). Your titles can differ — what should match is that each one states a takeaway, not a topic, and that reading all five in order makes a complete argument.

| Slide | Audience question | Takeaway title | Evidence/visual | Presenter action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why are we here? | Registrations skew toward first-time coders, and setup needs vary by district | One line: "72 registered, 25 may need a shared computer" | State the decision leadership must make: where to send setup support |
| 2 | What do the data show? | Mahabubnagar and Nizamabad have the highest beginner share and the largest device gaps | Bar chart: beginner share % per district, device gap per district | Point at the two highlighted bars, not the full table |
| 3 | Why does it matter? | Beginners without a personal device are the most likely to fall behind in a hands-on lab | Two numbers side by side: 83.3% beginner share vs. 3-device gap (Mahabubnagar) | Connect the two metrics explicitly — either alone understates the risk |
| 4 | What should we do? | Prioritize extra facilitators and shared devices for Mahabubnagar and Nizamabad | Simple recommendation card, not a spreadsheet | Ask for approval on facilitator allocation, not on the data itself |
| 5 | What happens next? | Approve two extra facilitators for the priority districts by Thursday | A single action + deadline | End on the ask, not a summary of everything shown |

## Why these titles, specifically

- Every title is a complete sentence with a claim in it ("skew toward," "have the highest," "are most likely," "prioritize," "approve") — none of them are a noun phrase like "Registration Data" or "District Breakdown."
- Slide 3 is the one people skip when building decks under time pressure, but it's the slide that turns two facts (83.3%, gap of 3) into a reason to act. Without it, slide 4's recommendation looks unmotivated.
- Slide 5 asks for one specific approval with a deadline, not "any questions?" — leadership should know exactly what saying yes commits them to.
