# Lab 2 — Example solution

One valid way to answer "Which workshop format should the organizing team choose, and why?" using only `source-pack.md`. Your table and wording can differ — what should match is that every claim traces to a labeled source.

## 1. Evidence table

| Claim | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Most participants want a hands-on, practice-heavy format | Source C (61 of 72 respondents prefer "more practice than lecture") | High |
| Device sharing will likely be required for some participants | Source A (72 registered) + Source B (40 desktops, laptops optional) | Medium — depends how many bring their own laptop |
| Every participant can rely on a personal paid AI subscription | — | Not established (Source D: "individual paid subscriptions are not guaranteed") |

## 2. Recommendation (under 120 words)

> Run the workshop as a hands-on lab with short instructor demos, not a lecture-heavy format. Source C shows 61 of 72 respondents (85%) prefer more practice than lecture, and only 3 prefer mostly lecture — the audience has already told us what works. Structure sessions around the 40 available desktops (Source B) as the shared baseline, with participants who bring laptops as a bonus rather than a requirement, since laptop ownership isn't confirmed for all 72 registrants (Source A). Do not assume individual paid AI subscriptions — Source D explicitly states these are not guaranteed, so every exercise needs a free-tier path. Confirm power outlet coverage before finalizing seating.

(107 words)

## 3. Two uncertainties that could change the recommendation

1. **Actual attendance vs. registration.** Source A gives registered count (72), not confirmed attendance — if turnout is significantly lower, the desktop-sharing math changes.
2. **Power outlet coverage.** Source B explicitly flags that outlets for every seat are unconfirmed — if outlets are limited, laptop users may be constrained regardless of how many bring one.

## 4. Facts that must not be inferred

- Expected attendance on the day (only registration count is known).
- Power availability at every seat (explicitly unconfirmed in Source B).
- Whether participants have paid AI subscriptions (explicitly not guaranteed in Source D).
- Individual accessibility needs (not covered by any source).
